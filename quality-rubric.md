# Quality Rubric — Tulip Registration/Confirmation Experience

Five YES/NO criteria for judging the quality of the registration/confirmation output — the
most important attendee-facing output in Tulip — grounded in what the confirmation page
actually shows: attendee name, event title, QR code, check-in code, event date/time/location,
and an instruction for what to do with it.

---

## 1. Attendee name and event title match exactly what was submitted.

The confirmation is the attendee's proof of registration — if it shows the wrong name or
event, it's not just unhelpful, it's actively wrong for whoever relies on it at the door.

---

## 2. Both a check-in code and a QR code are visibly present.

This is the actual check-in mechanism the app is built around — without both, staff have no
way to verify the attendee on event day, and the "confirmation" isn't functionally complete.

---

## 3. Event date, start/end time, and full location are all stated explicitly (not vaguely).

An attendee planning around this confirmation needs the concrete when/where — "sometime next
week" or "at the campus" isn't enough to actually show up on time.

---

## 4. An explicit instruction tells the attendee what to do with the confirmation (e.g. "show this code at check-in").

A confirmation with no instruction leaves the attendee holding a QR code with no idea what
happens next — this is the "useful next step" the assessment asks for.

---

## 5. The confirmation contains no claim that isn't actually true of the registration (e.g. no false payment confirmation, no incorrect status, no mention of a feature the app doesn't have).

This is the direct "no fabricated or misleading information" check — a confirmation is only
trustworthy if everything it asserts is real.

---

## Scoring

Score each criterion YES or NO independently. A response's overall result is the count of
YES out of 5.
